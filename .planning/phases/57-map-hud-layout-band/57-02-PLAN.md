<objective>
Turn the rail from a flex sibling that resizes the map into an absolutely-positioned overlay over the bottom of the play area.

Per D-01 (overlay, not a flex sibling; transform + visibility, never a display toggle) and D-02 (the viewport must not move — the stationary-camera ruling extends to the rail). This is the change Phase 58 (MOTION-01..05) depends on: a rail hidden with a display toggle cannot be animated, which is why the model changes now even though the transition itself is Phase 58's.

Purpose: LAYOUT-01. Today `#mw-rail` is a `flex:none; min-height:132px` child of `#app`'s flex column, hidden with a display rule — every show/hide steals ~132px from `.mw-screens` and therefore from `.mw-maze-viewport` (`flex:1`), so the whole map jumps up when a card appears and drops back when it clears.

Output: a new `#mw-stage` positioning wrapper, the rail's overlay CSS, and a structural proof that the rail can no longer affect the viewport's box.
</objective>

## Correction to CONTEXT's stated positioning parent — read before Task 1

D-01 names `.mazebox` as the rail's new positioning parent, and CONTEXT's "Existing Code Insights" repeats the 2026-09-19 todo's claim that `#mw-rail` is "a flex-column SIBLING after `.mazebox`" inside `#screen-maze`.

**Both are stale.** At HEAD the rail is a direct child of `#app`, between `<main class="mw-screens">` and `<nav class="mw-tabbar">` (`mazeworld.html:1616-1639`), and that position is deliberate: the 2026-09-17 UAT ruling recorded at `mazeworld.html:3736-3744` makes the rail the ONE feedback surface on EVERY tab. Moving it inside `.mazebox` would put it inside `#screen-maze`, which is `hidden` on the other four tabs — silently revoking that ruling.

The reflow mechanism CONTEXT describes is nonetheless exactly right; only the flex parent's name is wrong (`#app`, not `#screen-maze`). This plan therefore honours the locked intent — absolute overlay pinned to the bottom of the play area, transform-driven, never a flex sibling — by introducing a `#mw-stage` wrapper that spans exactly the region above the tab bar and contains both `.mw-screens` and `#mw-rail`. `bottom:0` on the rail then lands exactly on the top edge of the tab bar, with no need to know the tab bar's safe-area-dependent height.

Record this deviation in the plan SUMMARY.
</discovery>
