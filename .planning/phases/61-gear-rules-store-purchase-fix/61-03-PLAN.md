<objective>
STORE-02 in the engine: a store purchase always delivers what it charges for. `buyFrom` settles legality and room BEFORE any gold moves. A legal item that is not an upgrade is bought and stowed in the bag, and the rail says why it isn't an upgrade, using Plan 02's explanation (the STORE-03 rail-line clause). An upgrade still auto-equips, and its narration now names the traded-in piece. After that: measure, declare and regenerate the one fixture this moves (the economy script's Axe), and confirm the bot is unchanged.

Purpose: the user's Pixel 7 report: "It then does not show up in my inventory ... Make sure our store items are actually purchasable."

Implements CONTEXT "Store purchase always delivers (STORE-02)":
- Legal but not better goes to the bag.
- The replaced piece on an upgrade is unchanged: traded in, now narrated.
- Refusals happen before payment.
- The guarantee covers every store effect.

It also implements the "Specific Ideas" Spiked Staff acceptance (the rail line half; the store row half is Plan 04).

Design choices (Claude's discretion per CONTEXT):
- Pre-payment refusals REUSE the existing events: `buyFailed` for gold, `bagFull` for room, `itemRejected { reason }` for legality. Narration stays single-sourced per reason, and no refusal event types are added.
- The one new event is `purchaseBagged { item, why }`, carrying `why = gearCompareParts(c, it)` as plain data. Both narration tables format it through Plan 02's engine-free `upgradeWhyText`.
- `takeItem` keeps its prototype-faithful behaviour: its not-better branch is still the parity harness's legacy auto-take in `test/parity/harness/comparables.js#reconcilePendingFind/reconcilePendingLoot`. The store no longer reaches that branch, because `deliverGear` decides equip-vs-bag first. That is why the change moves only the economy fixture. Greenfield holds: the store has one path, not two.
</objective>
