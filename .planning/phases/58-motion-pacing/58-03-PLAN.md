**The camera model (quick task 260918-vm3).** `cam` is the maze-grid point under the viewport centre, in fractional cell units. It is never written to `S` and never persisted. `positionCanvas()` places the canvas from `cam`. `cameraPan()` gives the party's screen offset from the centre, which both the tap hit-test (`screenToCell`) and `positionPartyPulse` read. `keepPartyInView()` is the one non-centring nudge; `centerMap()` is the snap. The glide interpolates `cam` itself, frame by frame, through `applyCam`, so every existing reader (hit-test, ring, drag start) sees the displayed camera with no second transform (04-RESEARCH's "one shared transform" rule).

**Why retarget from the glide's target.** `keepInViewAxis` returns the current camera when the party is comfortably inside the margin. If the nudge were computed from a camera that is only halfway through its glide, a second step would return that halfway point and freeze the camera short of where it was heading. Computing from the in-flight TARGET keeps the destination stable, and the glide itself always starts from the displayed `cam`.

**Why the sandbox defaults to reduced motion.** `test/unit/harness/shellSandbox.js` stubs `matchMedia` as never-matching and its `setTimeout` never fires. Once shell effects are timed, a never-firing timer under "motion on" would strand every animated close, typing block and beat, and fail dozens of pre-existing tests for a harness reason rather than a real one. Defaulting the stub to reduced motion makes every existing sandbox test exercise the MOTION-05 end-state path, which is exactly the pre-phase behaviour. Animated-path tests opt in explicitly.
</discovery>
