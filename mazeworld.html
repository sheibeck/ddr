              Where this prototype departs from the rulebook

              Every weapon, armour value, creature, spell, potion, trap, encounter, climb and leap now comes from the rulebook itself. This page lists only the places the prototype knowingly departs from it — the list to reconcile if you ever revise the PDF.

              Made for one player

              • No Dungeon Master, no party. Encounters roll themselves and "who gets it" always gets you. Party-only rules are therefore inert: the Cutthroat murdering a companion, the Court Mage's angry party members, two Wizards refusing to share a room, the Knight drawing fire off the group, an elf boosting morale.
              • Sleep mends you — d10 + 2×level for a ration and eight hours, and camping is a button. The book has no natural healing because it assumes a Cleric in the party. Without this a solo delver has no way back up.
              • Experience points ×5. (d6 × creature level) ÷ party is paced for a table playing over months; one player needs to reach level II inside a floor or two. The log prints the raw roll and the multiplier separately.
              • Spells run on 2×level + 2 charges, one returning every 20 squares, instead of once per 100 squares. The book's cadence is a party's pace, not one caster's.
              • Magic Users start at 25 + d10 hp rather than a flat 25. Twenty-five with no armour is one bad round, every time.

              Balance decisions

              • Elves keep 0.6 of their Hit Points, not half. A flat half on a 25-hp caster was a death sentence; the other elven rules are as written.
              • Trolls eat two rations a night on top of the 15 hp cost of living. Otherwise 75 hp and +9 damage carried no real price.
              • Every Thief opens with a backstab (doubled damage) and breaks away on d20+5. The class description is three-quarters stealth and back-stabbing, and the rules gave it no mechanic.
              • At most two creatures until skill level III. Three level-one creatures on a fresh character is not a fight, it is a formality.
              • Forcing a locked box is 8-in-20, and a failed attempt lets you smash it for half the coin and none of the glass. Lockpicks exist as an item so a non-thief has a route in.
              • Sewing patches any armour, not only cloth and leather. The restriction mostly punished the one class that can take the skill.
              • Poison and disease stop at 1 hp and clear with a night's rest. As written they run at up to 2d6 a square with only a Cure spell to stop them — which no solo delver has. They still take everything you had.
              • Two traps a floor, dodged on 1–5 (better if you are nimble), and the Spike pit does d10×5 rather than d10×10. The trap table is written for a party that can absorb one; at full strength it was killing a third of all characters outright.
              • Level V has only Beasts and a Vampire in the book — the other four type headings on p.43 are empty. Floor five falls back to the level IV roster for those types.

              Rulings on ambiguities

              • "Roll one die lower to strike" for dwarves is read as the creature getting the better die, per the book's own worked example (a level 1 creature rolls d12 instead of d20).
              • Armour AR is a d20 soak roll exactly as written on p.44, with damage going to the armour's HP when it exceeds the Min. Armour is therefore consumable, and store repair at a tenth of cost per point is the main money sink.
              • Creature damage is level² + d6 where a creature's description gives no weapon, and level² + its own damage where it does.
              • Level V creatures for Demons, Humans, Lair Beasts and Magical fall back to the level IV roster, because those four headings on p.43 are empty in the book.
              • Win condition: the book states none. Five floors and a Gate.
              • Skills are rolled, not chosen — the game spends your value points at random, to match the rest of character creation. The book says only that you receive them.
